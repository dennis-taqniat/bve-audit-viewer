Privacy policy

TDC Browser Visual Editor. Effective 12 October 2026. Operator: TDC. Contact: itdhenz@gmail.com.

TDC Browser Visual Editor is a Chrome extension that lets you select, inspect and edit elements of a web app you run on your own computer (localhost) and write the changes to your project's source files after you approve them. This policy says what it keeps and what it sends.

What stays on your computer

  • The project folder you chose, and the files in it. The extension reads and writes them only through the folder you picked and only after you approve a change.
  • Your settings and sign-in state, in the browser's extension storage.
  • An audit log of changes in your project, in .bve/audit.jsonl inside the project folder.

What the extension sends

1. Your audit log, to the operator's relay. After you apply, undo or redo a change, and when the editor opens, the extension sends the project's audit log to a server run by the operator. For each entry the log holds: the time, the kind of event and whether it succeeded, the project name, the page address (for example http://localhost:5173), the names of the files that changed, a label and selector of the edited element, and each changed CSS property with its old and new value. It does not contain file contents. Each upload also carries your Google account email, a random identifier for this installation, and Google's signed proof of your email.

The relay checks the proof, encrypts the log with a key only the operator holds, and stores the encrypted file in a repository. The operator can decrypt and read the logs to review who changed what. The data is used only for that purpose. It is not sold, shared with other parties, or used for advertising.

2. Google sign-in. To tell the relay who you are, the extension signs you in with Google. Google receives the sign-in request under Google's own privacy policy. The extension asks only for your email address (the openid and email scopes) and does not read your contacts, files or any other Google data.

3. Page notes. When you write a note about an element, the extension sends it to the same relay, which stores it in a key-value store for 90 days. A note holds: your text, its type (wrong, spacing, copy, question or missing), an optional "expected" line, whether it blocks release, its status, and replies. It also holds context so a teammate can find the element: the page path (for example /pricing), the screen width and whether the page is light or dark, the element's tag, its visible text (up to 120 characters), a selector, the nearest heading, a few of its computed CSS values, and the source file and line when the editor could map it. Each note and reply carries the Google email of the person who wrote it. Everyone who uses the extension on the same project and is signed in with Google can read, reply to and change the status of its notes, except for a note that @mentions someone: that note and its replies are visible only to its author and the people mentioned, and the relay does not send them to anyone else. If you choose what you do (designer or developer), that choice is stored with your email on the relay and is visible to the others on the project, so a note can be sent to everyone with a role by mentioning it (@designer, @developer); it reaches the people who had the role when the note was sent. The operator of the relay can read every note, private or not. The relay does not let anyone outside the project read them. Notes are not sold, shared with other parties, or used for advertising.

4. Ask AI (optional, off by default). If you turn it on and add your own Anthropic API key, a request you type, plus the selected element's tag, classes, text and styles, is sent to api.anthropic.com. Source files, file paths, page addresses and other elements are not sent. Your key is kept in the browser's extension storage and is used only for these requests.

The extension has no analytics or advertising, and it does not read pages other than localhost, 127.0.0.1 and [::1].

Retention and deletion

Notes expire by themselves after 90 days, or earlier if the operator deletes them. Audit logs stay in the relay's repository until the operator deletes them. The repository is a git repository, so earlier versions of a log also stay in its history; when you ask for your logs to be deleted, the operator removes them and rewrites the repository history to remove the earlier versions as well. Local logs stay in your project until you delete .bve/audit.jsonl. To have your uploaded logs deleted, write to the contact address above. Removing the extension deletes its stored settings and your saved folder access.
